# Spirit-Beast Blades: The Bond Tournament

**Logline.** A lonely orphan who has never let anyone stay long enough to hurt them enters the legendary Bond Tournament to win a crown and a way out — and discovers that the only way to wake a sleeping spirit-beast, their own and their broken mentor's, is to risk the thing they fear most: letting people in. Standing in the final is a masked heir raised to believe love is the weakest blade of all — the person Rin would have become if no one had ever stayed.

**Total:** ~218 pages across 19 chapters.

## World

THE SPUN WORLD, capital CITY OF COILSPIRE. (Detailed cosmology, weaving system, politics, and geography are given below; the spirit/bond mechanics are detailed in spiritLore and the cast in characters.)

THE FIRST SPIN (founding cosmology, taught to every child as half-myth, half-physics): the world was made by a single blade of light turning at the heart of everything, throwing off sparks that cooled into spirit-beasts — fox, wolf, eagle, ten thousand kinds. Too vast to live in the world directly, the beasts learned to 'nest' in things that turn with intent: a top, a wheel, a mill, a falling coin. Because all beasts are sparks of ONE Spin, deeply bonded beasts can recognize each other as KIN across a dish — the mechanism that lets Rin's awakened beast wake the Coach's silenced one in the finale.

THE BLADES & THE RITUAL: spinners launch a battle-blade (always a 'blade,' never a 'toy' in-world) from a ripcord launcher, then weave the Six Signs to converse with the bonded beast. Keep the established launch ritual exactly: the three-two-one count and the cry 'Spirits, fly!' Under real emotion a beast can briefly MANIFEST as a towering radiant form over the dish — Rin's finale move.

POLITICS — CONCORD, THE WARDENS, THE TOURNAMENT: spinning is the world's great sport and quiet faith; cities organize around dish-halls and children apprentice to weaving-guilds. For a generation the powerful CONCORD GUILD has pushed efficient, sponsor-friendly cold-style 'modern weaving' and dismissed old bonded masters as superstitious, because cold-style spinners are cheaper to train and sell. The BOND TOURNAMENT, founded centuries ago, is the one arena where ONLY true bonds may compete, judged by BOND-WARDENS who can see whether a beast answers freely — but the Wardens are old and few, and Concord money has bought the judging council, so even the Tournament is eroding. Held every few years in the Coliseum of Coilspire as a brutal single-elimination bracket; the prize is the SPIRAL CROWN (said to be forged from the first cooled spark) plus enough money to be a real way out of the lower city.

THE BRACKET RIN CLIMBS: the GAUNTLET (open qualifier) → the ROUND OF THE THREE RIVALS (Kael, then Mira, then Brakk as the semifinal) → the GRAND FINAL against the Masked Ace, all over the political backdrop of Concord, the Wardens, and a threatened rigged final.

GEOGRAPHY & CULTURE: Coilspire and its Coliseum; the quiet hill-town the Coach retired to; and the lower city's orphan DISH-HALLS where Rin grew up spinning a chipped, no-name, hand-me-down blade nobody else wanted. Running motif and the book's last line: 'A blade wobbles when it spins alone, and steadies when something turns alongside it.'

CONTINUITY NOTE FOR WRITERS: the playable vertical-slice (Kael duel) and the larger storyboarded comic both already use the established beats — 'Spirits, fly!', the three-two-one count, the spirit-weaving hand-signs, the f_reveal/f_unite/f_awaken finale structure, and the three flags. Enrich these; do not replace them. Rin is the named, faceless reader-insert protagonist (front views stay implied/POV; rin_back is the only Rin sprite). The three scoring flags are set in exactly three chapters (Kael=calm|bold, Mira=kind|fierce, Brakk=steady|reckless); all earlier/extra choices are tactical or tone flavor and must NOT set scoring flags. Endings: all-heart→Champion of Heart, all-fire→Champion of Fire, any mix→Heart of the Blade.

## Spirit Lore

A spirit-beast is a fragment of the First Spin — a living current of will that nests in anything turning with intent. In a blade it shows as a faint beast-shape in the blur; under strong emotion it can MANIFEST as a towering radiant ghost-form (the TRUE FORM). Each beast has a TEMPER (its nature) and a NAME it gives only to a spinner it truly trusts; an unbonded blade holds a beast that has not yet given its name.

BONDING IS BEING HEARD, NOT CAPTURED — a two-way promise sealed by being truly known. When a spinner first wakes a core, they feel the beast's defining emotion as their own. A blade is a two-way mirror of its spinner, so the beasts heal as the spinners do: Kael's wolf GREYWIND carries the loneliness of a pack-animal bred to be a lonely weapon and over-attacks because Kael does; Mira's nine-tailed fox SUMI is wary, read-and-used by handlers its whole life, never chosen; Brakk's storm-eagle STONEWING is a vast gentle thing that only ever learned to roar — and theirs is the oldest, deepest bond in the bracket, the book's living model of a true bond. A bond can grow, be wounded, and — the central tragedy — be SILENCED.

SPIRIT-WEAVING is the Six Signs (Listen, Steady, Turn, Open, Hold, Release), taught as a duet, not control. RELEASE, with a deep enough bond, calls the true form. OPEN is the hardest sign and the spine of Rin's arc — it does not command the beast, it lets the beast in, and cannot be faked; deep mastery of it is the near-lost 'open-hand weave.' COLD SIGNS are the dark mirror: signs altered to COMPEL rather than ask. They grant instant power and slowly DEAFEN the beast to its spinner — a cold-style master can beat almost anyone while going quietly deaf to the spirit that powers them. Vehesal perfected cold signs; Raze is his heir, holding his own snow-leopard one degree from the Silence.

THE SILENCE: a beast spun out by a cold sign of sufficient cruelty can retreat so deep it never wakes, leaving a blade that will never spin true again. The world's great taboo; whispered to be a trapping, not a death; never undone by anyone — until this story.

THE TRUE FORM cannot be forced, bought, or trained. It wakes only when a spinner is WHOLLY SINCERE and NOT ALONE — when the bond reaches past the single spinner to everyone they've truly let in, in real extremity (at risk of losing the people they love, not merely to win). This is why Rin cannot win on skill: the true form needs Kael, Mira, and Brakk to be real and needs Rin to finally believe they will stay. Rin's chipped no-name beast is the hardest in the world to wake precisely because it has never given its name — and it never has because Rin has never believed anyone would stay; so its blaze, and the name it finally speaks, is the thesis made visible.

THE SILENCE'S CURE (the finale's deepest reveal): a silenced beast can be reached by another bond freely offered. The Coach's elk wakes not because he wins but because Rin's newly-manifest beast calls to it as KIN — both sparks of the same First Spin (the cosmology pays off here). The same offered bond reaches Raze's all-but-silenced snow-leopard, which answers for the first time in years. Cold dominance can win a thousand matches but never reach this deepest thing — the hollow at Raze's center, the secret rot in Vehesal's creed (he has never once woken a true form), and the crack Rin's bond pours into.

## Themes

Belonging versus self-protection. The book's quiet thesis: a bond is not a weakness you can be punished for, but the only thing strong enough to wake what has gone silent inside us — and inside the people we love. Rin's wound is abandonment; Raze's is the same wound turned to armor; the Coach's is grief calcified into silence; Vehesal's is grief mistaken for wisdom and built into a doctrine. Every duel is really a conversation about whether it is safe to need other people. Secondary threads: legacy as a thing you CHOOSE, not merely inherit (the Coach and Vehesal are two endpoints of the same loss; Rin and Raze two endpoints of the same wound; Rin and Pae the cycle turned forward); the difference between fire that warms and fire that scorches; the difference between winning and being worthy; and grief as something you carry forward rather than get over. The cold-style/Concord plot makes the theme political as well as personal: a whole culture is choosing dominance over connection, and the Tournament is the last place that remembers why connection matters. The interactive flags (calm/kind/steady vs bold/fierce/reckless) are NEVER framed as right vs wrong — both heart and fire are honored; a whole person needs some of each, which is exactly why the blended ending (Heart of the Blade) is the rarest and most earned.

## Voice & Reading Level

AUDIENCE & LEVEL: upper middle-school to high-school (ages ~12-17), at the prose level of The Hunger Games, the later Percy Jackson books, or His Dark Materials. American English. Sophisticated but clear vocabulary (trust the reader; use precise lore words — manifest, doctrine, cold sign, the Silence, open-hand weave — and define them in-scene through action, never with glossary asides).

POV & INTERIORITY: third person limited, close on Rin, literary but propulsive. Give real interiority — Rin's thoughts, the physical 'wobble in the chest' of fear and hope, memories that surface mid-scene, the habit of counting exits and narrating other people's faces for early warning signs of rejection. The reader should FEEL Rin's loneliness without ever being told 'Rin was lonely.' Subtext over statement; let the prose hold the gap between what characters say and mean.

SENTENCE CRAFT: vary structure hard. Long, breath-held sentences during the slow burn of a duel or a reflection; then a hard short line on impact. No sing-song, repetitive, picture-book cadence; no 'And then... and then...'.

SENSORY SPECIFICITY: the smell of hot metal and chalk dust in a dish-hall; the particular ring a blade makes when its spin is dying; the cold under-feeling of a forced sign; the silence of a held breath in a packed stadium; the weight of a dead-quiet core.

EMOTIONAL MATURITY, NOT GORE: stakes are loss, grief, belonging, doubt, legacy, the fear of being alone and the fear of being left. The Silence is genuinely frightening as an idea — a friend who goes quiet forever — with no blood or cruelty-for-its-own-sake. Battles are tense and kinetic but never gory; they are EMOTIONAL ARGUMENTS FIRST and physics second — every clash means something about the relationship.

DIALOGUE is character-specific: Kael performs and over-talks; Mira measures, clean and economical; Brakk booms warmth; Raze is clipped, formal, and quietly sad under the doctrine; the Coach speaks in plain, weighted understatement; Pae is eager and unguarded; Vehesal is courtly, persuasive, never cruel — the scariest kind of villain.

THE CHOICES must read as genuine in-character expressions of the reader's Rin and as two valid ways to be a good person under pressure — NEVER good vs bad. calm/bold, kind/fierce, steady/reckless are all honorable; the prose must respect whichever the reader picks and never tell them how to feel. Battle choices (unleash/counter/defend) are real tactics with distinct emotional flavor, not reskins. Extra choices outside the three rival chapters are flavor/tone only and set no scoring flag.

CRAFT TARGETS: roughly 220-320 words of real prose per 'page,' several pages per chapter (see per-chapter page counts; ~218 pages total across 19 chapters). Foreshadow early (cold signs, the Silence, Rin's no-name beast, the open-hand weave, the kinship of beasts) and pay off late — EARN the finale. Manga pages land only at peak beats; the prose does the heavy character lifting between them. Avoid slang that will date.

## Characters

### Rin — Protagonist (they/them), 15-year-old orphan challenger from the lower-city dish-halls

- **Backstory:** Left at a lower-city orphan dish-hall as an infant; adopted out and returned twice, then aged out — Rin learned early that wanting to stay was the surest way to be sent away, so they made themselves small, useful, and unattached, and got very good at counting the exits in any room. At nine they kept a chipped, no-name, hand-me-down battle-blade nobody else wanted, not as a weapon but as the one thing no one would take back — and it was the first thing that never left. It answered them, faintly, in a way it answered no one, because Rin talked to it like the only friend they had. But it has never given its name, and Rin privately reads that as proof the bond is broken, that nothing stays. The Coach found them spinning alone at dawn and, recognizing something lost in the world, trained them in secret.
- **Motivation:** Consciously: win the crown and the prize money — a real, concrete way out of the lower city — and prove a tailings kid belongs. Truly: to find out whether they are someone worth keeping, and to keep, at last, the one bond that has never abandoned them.
- **Flaw:** Rin pre-emptively withholds their heart. They make rivals into friends easily, because friendship-at-arm's-length is safe; what terrifies them is letting anyone close enough that losing them would hurt. They will help everyone and lean on no one — and that is the exact thing that keeps their true form, and their beast's name, asleep.
- **Arc:** From a guarded loner who survives by needing nothing and keeps every win at arm's length, to a champion who wins only by finally, openly needing the people who love them — which is the precise condition that wakes their beast's name and true form. The reader's flags color HOW Rin opens up (warm/steady vs daring/fierce), never WHETHER.

### The Coach (Tovan, once 'the Spirit-Sage') — Rin's mentor; the last true Bond-Sage, a former greatest-champion-alive carrying a silenced blade

- **Backstory:** A generation ago, Tovan and his bonded elk were unbeatable — the last true Bond-Sage. His dearest friend and equal, Vehesal, grew obsessed with winning, perfected cold signs, and in their final duel spun Tovan's blade out so cruelly that Tovan's elk went SILENT and never woke. Tovan blamed himself — not for losing, but for caring more about beating his friend than reaching him — quit, and hid for twenty years in a hill-town, telling no one who he had been and carrying a silent blade he can neither wake nor bring himself to bury. Then Rin walked into his line of sight, talking to a chipped no-name blade like a friend.
- **Motivation:** To pass on the dying true-bonding art and to give Rin the one thing he failed to give Vehesal — living proof that a bond is worth more than a crown — and, though he can barely admit it, to hear his own beast's voice one more time before he dies.
- **Flaw:** He confuses protecting people with keeping the truth from them — he hides his past, hides Vehesal's and Raze's connection, and hides his own fragile hope, because hoping again terrifies him. His silence nearly costs Rin the final.
- **Arc:** From a grief-frozen man who buried his heart with his silent beast to one who lets it ache again. He does not get his old glory back; he gets something better — a student who surpasses the cost he paid, and, in the finale, his own long-silent elk woken (not by his victory but by Rin's manifest calling to it as kin). He forgives himself at last.

### Kael — First rival; aggressive silver WOLF blade (Greywind); becomes Rin's first true friend

- **Backstory:** A brilliant, swaggering Gauntlet qualifier from a struggling outer-borough club. The youngest son of a famous, frosty blade-house where love is performance-reviewed, he was groomed since childhood and has never once been told 'well done.' His cockiness is a costume over a boy who has never been allowed to lose and is therefore secretly terrified of it — and terrified that the Concord scouts will pass him over. His wolf Greywind is loyal and hot-headed exactly like him, and over-attacks because Kael fights to outrun the quiet moment where he might be found wanting.
- **Motivation:** Consciously, to win a father's approval that does not exist and to belong in the same arena as the prodigies; after meeting Rin, to deserve the friendship he was freely given and to find out who he is when he is simply allowed to love the game.
- **Flaw:** Performs a confidence he does not feel; mistakes being feared for being valued, and domination for worth — the early, redeemable shadow of the Cold creed.
- **Arc:** Loses to Rin and, instead of breaking, finds the first rival who treated him as a person rather than an obstacle — and becomes Rin's loudest, most fiercely loyal friend, the first person who chooses Rin and stays, which cracks Rin's armor open. Later refuses a Concord cold-style offer because of what he saw in Rin's bond. His lonely-weapon wolf finds a pack.

### Mira — Second rival; brilliant, reserved nine-tailed FOX strategist (Sumi); becomes friend and the story's investigator

- **Backstory:** A Concord-trained prodigy raised on efficient modern weaving — the youngest spinner ever to read an opponent's whole pattern — valued for her reads, not her heart, and taught that feelings are noise to filter out. Her fox Sumi is precise and reserved exactly like her. Mira has studied every match Rin ever spun and assumes she has solved Rin completely, until Rin does the one thing she cannot model: caring more about the opponent than the win.
- **Motivation:** Started out to prove precision beats passion and to be surprised for once by someone she cannot reduce to a pattern; ends up trying to save the very thing precision was erasing.
- **Flaw:** Substitutes analysis for intimacy; knows everyone and is close to no one (Rin's wound mirrored from the opposite side). Afraid that if she stops being the smartest person in the room, she is nothing.
- **Arc:** Defeated by the variable she filtered out, and instead of resenting it she is moved — the girl who reads everyone asks Rin to teach her the one thing she cannot calculate: how to bond. She becomes Rin's strategist and conscience, and the first to recognize the micro-tells of cold signs in Raze's spinning — making her the detective who exposes that the Masked Ace is going deaf to his own beast and that Concord intends to rig the final. Her used-and-read fox learns to be chosen.

### Brakk — Third rival / semifinal; thunderous gentle-giant powerhouse with a mighty storm-EAGLE blade (Stonewing); becomes the warm anchor of the found family

- **Backstory:** The largest spinner in the league and the kindest soul in the bracket, from a hard mountain quarry-town where his size made everyone assume he was a brute — so he learned to be loud and boastful, because that was the only role the world ever offered him. His thunder-eagle Stonewing has been bonded to him since childhood; theirs is the oldest, deepest bond in the bracket. He loves the fight for the pure joy of it, not to dominate, and is privately lonely that people fear his strength instead of meeting it.
- **Motivation:** To find an opponent who will give him their absolute everything and meet him as a person rather than a wall — to be MET, fully, for once; by the end, content simply to be wanted as a friend.
- **Flaw:** Hides a tender heart behind thunderous bravado and undervalues himself as 'just strong'; secretly fears that the day he can't win by sheer power is the day no one will want him around.
- **Arc:** His semifinal with Rin is the book's tender emotional core — two people who both quietly believed strength is the only thing that makes them lovable. Rin tells him the thing he has never heard (that he'd be wanted even if he lost) and the giant is undone by it. He becomes the first to step forward and stand with Rin at the finale, and the one who literally and emotionally holds the others up. His eagle, who only ever learned to roar, finally learns to soar.

### Raze — Final antagonist; the Masked Ace — undefeated cold-sign heir and student of Vehesal and the Cold creed

- **Backstory:** Taken in as a small child by Vehesal and raised on a single doctrine: bonds are leashes, need is weakness, dominance is the only safety. Raze has never been allowed a friend, a loss, or a soft word; the mask is not for mystery but because Vehesal taught him a champion must never show a face an enemy can read. He is the strongest spinner of his generation and utterly alone — a cold-sign master who keeps his own snow-leopard (whose name he can no longer remember) in a forced silence one degree from the Silence itself. He is, though he would die before admitting it, exactly what Rin would have become if no one had ever stayed.
- **Motivation:** Consciously: to prove the Cold creed correct by crushing the most bonded spinner alive and win the crown for Concord — because if bonds really are strength, then everything done to him, and everything he gave up, was a lie he cannot survive looking at. Unconsciously: to be proven wrong, because some buried part of him is screaming under the silence he keeps his beast in.
- **Flaw:** Has armored his abandonment wound into a philosophy and calls his loneliness strength. He genuinely believes he is sparing Rin the grief that bonds always end in. His cold blade has never once woken a true form — he does not even know such a thing exists — and that hollow at his center is the crack Rin's bond pours into.
- **Arc:** From the unbeatable, unreachable Masked Ace to a boy cracked open by witnessing a bond he cannot explain away — Rin's manifest, the Coach's elk waking, and his own buried snow-leopard answering faintly. His defeat is not a humiliation but a rescue: for the first time in years he hears his own beast. Rin crosses the dish and offers a hand — not pity, recognition: 'You were alone. You don't have to be.' He is reached, not redeemed in a tidy bow; the ending leaves him estranged from Vehesal and tentatively reaching, alone, toward the friends he denied himself — the seed of his own path.

### Vehesal, the Frost-Sage — The hidden architect; the Coach's lost friend, founder of cold-style spinning, and the grey power behind Concord and Raze — the true shadow over the book

- **Backstory:** Once Tovan's dearest friend and equal, Vehesal lost a single bonded match he was certain he deserved to win and concluded that bonds had betrayed him — that caring is just a lever others use to break you. He severed his own bond on purpose, perfected cold signs, and beat Tovan so cruelly that Tovan's elk never woke again. He has spent twenty years building Concord's cold-style takeover of the sport and raising Raze in his own wound, and he has never told a living soul his ugliest secret: in twenty years of cold victories he has never once woken a true form — and he too has gone deaf to his own beast and is terrified of the quiet. He no longer enters a dish himself; he fights through Raze.
- **Motivation:** To win, finally and publicly, the argument he lost to Tovan thirty years ago — that bonds are weakness — so the loss that broke him can stop hurting; to make the whole world cold so that his own coldness stops feeling like a wound.
- **Flaw:** His entire philosophy is a scar he refuses to look at — he mistook grief for wisdom, built a doctrine on it, and cannot admit the bond he severed was his own.
- **Arc:** The counterpoint to the Coach: where Tovan let his grief soften him at last, Vehesal let his harden into a creed. He is never fought in a duel — Rin never faces him — but defeated in MEANING: his own student feels a bond he swore was impossible, and for one terrible instant in the stands Vehesal hears his own long-silenced beast and turns away. His certainty shatters; his control over Raze breaks. He leaves the book undone rather than destroyed — the cautionary endpoint Rin's world is fighting not to become. No redemption.

### Pae — Supporting; Rin's younger friend from the orphan dish-hall — the human stakes of home and the next turn of the cycle

- **Backstory:** A younger kid from Rin's old orphanage who idolizes Rin and follows them to the capital, spinning a wobbly beginner blade. Pae is everything Rin used to be: hungry to be kept, terrified of being left behind. Where Rin holds people at arm's length, Pae simply refuses to be held there.
- **Motivation:** To not be left behind — the same wound as Rin, one turn younger — and to stay near the first person who ever made them feel kept.
- **Flaw:** Idolizes Rin and has to learn that Rin is allowed to be afraid too; covers a soft heart by trying to be brave before they're ready.
- **Arc:** Gives Rin someone to protect, which tethers Rin to where they came from; and by the end gives Rin someone to MENTOR the way the Coach mentored them — closing the generational loop. The proof, in the stands and at the final dish, that Rin was never starting from zero.

### Warden Oru — Supporting; the eldest Bond-Warden, keeper of the Tournament's old law and the honest judge

- **Backstory:** The last Bond-Warden who can still clearly see a true bond — half-blind in the eyes and wholly seeing in the way that matters. Outvoted and outspent by the Concord-leaning council, she has stayed quiet too long and waited years for a spinner whose bond is real enough to remind the world what the Tournament was for.
- **Motivation:** To die having seen one more true bond crowned — and, in the final, to stop Concord rigging it.
- **Flaw:** Let cynicism keep her silent too long; mistakes endurance for stewardship until Rin gives her a reason to act.
- **Arc:** From a marginalized old judge to the one who, at the climax, invokes the Tournament's oldest law so the bond will be judged honestly — letting the real fight matter. The establishment itself, moved despite itself.

### Ch 1: The Chipped Blade  (~11 pages)
Open in the lower-city orphan dish-hall, not the arena. Establish Rin's wound, the chipped no-name blade, Pae, and the day everything changes — grounding the reader in the world Rin came from before any tournament.

_Beats:_
  - Dawn in a worn lower-city dish-hall: Rin spins the chipped, no-name hand-me-down blade alone — the one place the 'wobble in the chest' goes quiet. Establish the abandonment backstory in lived sensory detail (chalk dust, hot metal, counting exits), never exposition.
  - Establish the no-name beast: it answers Rin faintly but has never given its name; Rin privately reads this as proof the bond is broken, that nothing stays. Plant the central mystery.

He offers to train Rin for the Bond Tournament; names the prize money as a real way out but hints the real prize is something else. Rin, guarded, almost refuses out of habit — and doesn't.
  - World seed: faded Concord 'modern weaving' posters, an old Tournament banner, the first whisper of 'cold signs' as the thing winners use now. Rin holds the dead-quiet blade and decides to go.
